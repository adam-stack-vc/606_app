Not that long ago, "high-frequency trading" was a dirty word on Wall Street — code for secretive enterprises that leveraged microwave towers, fiber optic cables, and other expensive hardware to poach trades and front-run other investors, reaping millions in the process.


Today, these "Flash Boys" — as they were dubbed in the 2014 book by Michael Lewis  — are among the most powerful and influential trading juggernauts in the markets. The controversy that once engulfed them has largely subsided, with the notable exception of those that pay retail brokers like Robinhood for customer trade orders, a practice called payment-for-order-flow that's garnered a stern look from regulators and lawmakers over the past year.

They're also recognized as critical liquidity providers in a variety of asset classes, including equities, fixed-income, derivatives, ETFs, and, increasingly, digital assets. They're privately owned and, unlike hedge funds, don't manage outside money. They still guard their inner workings as if characters in a John Le Carré spy novel, but some prop traders have dropped their veil of secrecy a smidge as their heft has ballooned.


Citadel Securities Chief Executive Officer Peng Zhao, for example, recently talked to Bloomberg in his first news interview ever about his rise through the ranks. "You get to a point where staying under the radar is no longer an option," he said of his decision to speak. "We want to be able to tell our own story, rather than having the story told about us."

"High-frequency trading" has lost some pejorative sting as these firms have become dominant fixtures in various global markets. Still, these firms usually prefer the more staid monikers "market maker" or "proprietary trading firm" — in part because they find the emphasis on velocity reductive, like highlighting a Tesla's acceleration at the expense of its other engineering and design feats.

Speed, down to the billionths of a second, remains a crucial competitive advantage. But as these firms continue their expansion into new asset classes, including cryptocurrencies, and longer trading horizons, novel research and cutting-edge trading algorithms are an equally powerful weapon.


"Our ability to make money is based on our research and quantitative horsepower and data science, not our speed," David Olsen, president and chief investment officer at Chicago-based Jump Trading, told [The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/articles/high-speed-trading-firm-jump-to-execute-retail-investors-stock-trades-11628760601).

As a result, prop trading firms are voracious hirers of engineers, data scientists, and quantitative traders and researchers. They fight to bring aboard the most promising Ph.D. holders graduating each year in hard sciences, competing not just with systematic hedge funds but also elite Silicon Valley corporations.

Given the intense competition, many look beyond the US borders to hire brilliant technologists. When US companies file paperwork for visas on behalf of current or prospective foreign workers, they're required to say how much base compensation the workers are offered. And every year, [the Office of Foreign Labor Certification discloses this salary data in an enormous dataset](https://www.foreignlaborcert.doleta.gov/performancedata.cfm).


Insider analyzed the agency's 2021 disclosure data for permanent and temporary foreign workers to shed light on what these proprietary trading firms paid for talent last year. These figures do not include bonuses, which typically comprise a substantial portion of total compensation.

According to US Department of Labor documentation, the offered wages in the disclosure data are the minimum amounts companies provided in foreign labor certification applications for specific workers.

The wages are derived from the average compensation that similar employees are paid in a given job and industry, known as the "prevailing wage." Prevailing wage sets a floor for their salary, but salaries are commonly higher than the prevailing wage.


In addition to Citadel Securities and Jump, other top market makers on the list include: DRW, Hudson River Trading, Jane Street, Susquehanna International Group, and Tower Research. Virtu, one of the largest HFTs handling retail stock trades, is a public company and wasn't included on the list.

#### Here are the salaries for the most common trading, engineering, and research roles at the top US prop trading firms.

Citadel Securities
------------------

![Citadel Securities trader on the floor of NYSE]()


Citadel Securities hired more than 150 people using H-1B visa last year, including 49 quantitative researchers, 23 quantitative research analysts, 19 software engineers, and 16 traders.
Brendan McDermid/Reuters





**Headquarters:** Chicago

**Employees:** 1,300+


Launched in 2001, the market-making arm of Ken Griffin's financial empire has often resided in the shadow of its older hedge fund brother, Citadel.

That dynamic has shifted in recent years, in part because of its size. Citadel Securities is now among the largest institutions executing trades in options and equities, handling [one-quarter of the US equities](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/products/equities-and-options/) volume across a broad swath of the market and handling more trades from individual investors than any other firm. But it's also grown into a major player in ETFs, interest rate swaps, US treasuries, and foreign currencies, doing business with more than 1,600 financial institutions.

That expansion has attracted attention — including from marquee investors. After producing a record $7 billion in revenues last year, the firm in January [secured a $1.15 billion investment](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/citadel-securities-valued-22-bln-after-investment-sequoia-paradigm-2022-01-11/) from Sequoia Capital and Paradigm at a $22 billion valuation. Both VCs are aggressively involved in crypto, signaling a shift for Griffin, one of the most vocal critics of digital assets.


But the electronic trading giant also gained infamy during the GameStop short-squeeze in 2021, becoming a boogeyman among conspiracy-minded retail traders for its close ties to Robinhood, the popular no-fee trading app. Citadel Securities, and other top market makers, pay retail brokers like Robinhood [hundreds of millions](https://markets.businessinsider.com/news/stocks/stock-brokers-made-billions-pfof-gamestop-craze-robinhood-charles-schwab-2022-2) in fees for the right to execute their clients' stock and options trades.

As one of the largest prop trading firms, Citadel Securities is unsurprisingly the most frequent employer of H-1B visa professionals. Last year it used the H-1B visa program to hire more than 150 people, including 49 quantitative researchers, 23 quantitative research analysts, 19 software engineers, and 16 traders.

DRW
---

![Techologists at DRW office]()


DRW hired four quantitative trading analysts and three software developers using H-1B visas last year.
DRW/Glassdoor





**Headquarters:** Chicago


**Employees:** 1,250+

One of the oldest prop trading firms on the block, DRW was founded in 1992 by its namesake Donald Wilson, then a young fixture of the Chicago Mercantile Exchange's live trading pits. The firm started out trading Eurodollar options with an eye toward exploiting technology and the rise of electronic trading. It has steadily expanded its remit since, with offices and systematic trading strategies spanning the globe focused on commodities, FX, ETFs, and cryptocurrencies.

DRW was one of the earliest financial institutions to embrace bitcoin, mining and trading the digital asset through its Cumberland subsidiary starting in 2014. As acceptance has spread, it's become one of the [cryptocurrency's leading market makers](https://www.wsj.com/articles/bitcoins-trading-star-is-chicago-high-speed-firm-that-nods-to-the-grateful-dead-1511787600), and in 2021 it [executed Goldman Sachs'](https://twitter.com/drwtrading/status/1390411496295895054?lang=en) first-ever [crypto block trade](https://webcache.googleusercontent.com/search?q=cache:SY-tvTglIIEJ:https://news.bloomberglaw.com/banking-law/goldman-offers-new-bitcoin-derivatives-to-wall-street-investors+&cd=3&hl=en&ct=clnk&gl=us) in the form of bitcoin futures.


In 2020, amid a pandemic-induced spike in trading, DRW earned revenues of $1.5 billion and earnings before taxes, depreciation, and amortization of $750 million, according to [a Bloomberg report](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-03-14/ex-d-e-shaw-research-quant-spins-his-crypto-firm-into-unicorn).

DRW hired the fewest employees via H-1B visa on this list, with just 21 in total. That included four quantitative trading analysts and three software developers.

Hudson Securities made 92 H-1B visa hires last year, trailing only the much larger Citadel Securities and Jump Trading.
HRT/Glassdoor

Hudson River Trading bills itself as a math-focused trading enterprise, with a mantra of, ["Built by coders. Led by coders."](https://www.hudsonrivertrading.com/about/) The smallest firm on this list by headcount, HRT nonetheless hired a slew of quants, engineers, and developers through H-1B visa last year — 92 in all, which trails only the much larger Citadel Securities and Jump Trading. The most common roles it hired on visa were algorithm developer (21), core developer (14), and algorithm engineer (11).

That dovetails with an overall hiring spurt for the firm, whose headcount more than doubled over the past two years, according to LinkedIn data.


Founded in 2002, HRT handles about 8% of the US daily stock-trading volume, but it also develops algorithms to trade in derivatives, FX, fixed-income, and, like several of its competitors, cryptocurrencies.

The firm [last summer revealed intentions to compete](https://www.wsj.com/articles/high-frequency-trader-hudson-river-to-execute-retail-stock-trades-11625047200) with Citadel Securities, Virtu, and several others that execute giant volumes of trades for individual investors through online retail brokerages.

Jane Street
-----------

![Jane Street office 2]()


Jane Street used the H-1B program last year to hire 14 software engineers and seven traders, as well as two exotic options traders.
Jane Street





**Headquarters:** New York City


**Employees:** 1,200+

Jane Street trades across many of same asset classes as its chief HFT rivals, from stocks and options to cryptocurrencies. But its bread and butter is exchange-traded funds, trading trillions in ETF volume annually as one of the largest market makers in the world.

Jane Street has a stranglehold on the bond ETF market in particular, coupling an ultra-fast algorithmic approach with human trading intuition and [holding positions at times for days or longer](https://www.ft.com/content/81811f27-4a8f-4941-99b3-2762cae76542) — an eternity compared with the traditional standards of high-frequency trading.


It's been an enormously profitable niche. Jane Street reaped $10.6 billion in net trading revenues and $7.8 billion in ebitda in its fiscal year that ended in March of 2021, according to [Bloomberg,](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-06-18/jane-street-and-drw-traders-made-billions-in-pandemic-markets) thanks in part to trading volatility stoked by the pandemic.

The firm was founded 2000 by defectors from Susquehanna International Group and maintains an offbeat identity, according to a 2021 [profile in the Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/81811f27-4a8f-4941-99b3-2762cae76542). It has no CEO or official management committee, but instead "resembles an anarchist commune, informally led by a group of 30 or 40 senior executives," according to the FT.

The market-maker also programs [almost exlusively in the esoteric OCaml](https://www.janestreet.com/technology/) coding language.


The company used the H-1B program last year to hire 14 software engineers and seven traders, as well as two exotic options traders, who trade options contracts with nontraditional structures or customized risk features.

Jump Trading
------------

![jump trading office]()


Jump Street was was the second-most prolific consumer of H-1B visas last year with 95.
Jump Trading





**Headquarters:** Chicago

**Employees:** 1,100+


For most of its 20-year existence, Jump Trading has epitomized the dual obsessions with speed and secrecy that have come to typify high-frequency trading. The firm has always been on the vanguard in the hunt for trading connections approaching light speed, whether that's via fiber-optic cables, [microwave towers](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2019-03-08/the-gazillion-dollar-standoff-over-two-high-frequency-trading-towers), or [hollow-core glass tubes](https://www.wsj.com/articles/high-frequency-traders-push-closer-to-light-speed-with-cutting-edge-cables-11608028200?mod=article_inline).

That speed combined with a vigorous pursuit of the smartest engineers and quant researchers in the world has made Jump one of the fiercest market-makers in the industry across several asset classes — and it's accomplished it with monk-like quietude, up until recently averting public attention at every turn.

Jump's website, for instance, could be confused for a Y2K relic — static, containing a few modest hyperlinks, most prominently one to its job board. Another directs browsers to the company's [crypto initiatives,](https://jumpcrypto.com/) where Jump is not bashful at all.


The company is one of the most active participants in crypto markets, covertly launching those efforts in 2015. The company's extroverted subsidiary formally launched last fall — with a [real live press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210914005700/en/Jump-Trading-Group-Launches-Jump-Crypto) and all — and the staff of roughly 140 have taken to blogging, [podcasting](https://jumpcap.com/insights/the-institutionalization-of-crypto), [press interviews](https://www.theblockcrypto.com/post/135044/jump-tradings-dave-olsen-on-the-key-building-blocks-for-a-crypto-market-maker), and colorful presentations where they seek to answer the time-honored question: ["Who the F\*\*k Is Jump?"](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2022/03/25/jump-crypto-wants-you-to-know-its-name/)

What broke the quiet prop trader out of its shell? It may help that cryptocurrencies are still nascent, growing markets and not nearly as competitive for trading profits as traditional asset classes. But also, Jump Crypto isn't just a trading firm; it's also investing in start-ups, and half of its developers are dedicated to building its own protocols, [according to CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2022/03/25/jump-crypto-wants-you-to-know-its-name/).

Its expansion has required steady hiring, and it was the second-most prolific consumer of H-1B visas last year with 95, most frequently using them to bring aboard quantitative researchers (27), software engineers (25), and quantitative developers (12).

Susquehanna International Group
-------------------------------

![Office at Susquehanna International Group headquarters]()


Susquehanna International Group hired 67 roles via the H-1B program last year, including 24 software developers.
Susquehanna International Group/Glassdoor





**Headquarters:** Bala Cynwyd, Pennsylvania

**Employees:** 2,400+

Susquehanna International Group isn't just the largest of the US prop trading firms on this list, it's the oldest, founded in 1987 by Jeff Yass. And unlike its peers, it isn't headquartered in Chicago or NYC, but rather just outside Philadelphia in the suburb of Bala Cynwyd.


That's where Yass in the early 1980s got his start as an options trader on the Philadelphia Stock Exchange and a protege of hedge fund billionaire Izzy Englander, who funded his early trading, according [to a profile in Forbes Magazine](https://www.forbes.com/sites/antoinegara/2021/04/06/how-trader-jeff-yass-parlayed-poker-and-horse-racing-bets-into-a-12-billion-fortune/?sh=41d2470d3eb5). But he cut his teeth making real bets as a professional poker player and horse bettor — he and some friends were once banned from a race track outside Chicago for exploiting a promotional wager that netted them hundreds of thousands of dollars.

To this day the firm recruits and trains employees using poker tournaments — three SIG traders have won tournaments at the World Series of Poker, according to Forbes.

The firm trades in all products, but looms especially large in the derivatives business — it has by some measures been the largest trader [of US-listed stock options in the world](https://www.forbes.com/sites/antoinegara/2021/04/06/how-trader-jeff-yass-parlayed-poker-and-horse-racing-bets-into-a-12-billion-fortune/?sh=41d2470d3eb5) — ETFs, and retail stock trading.


SIG has become a prominent venture and private equity investor as well, and it recently launched a business deploying its quant-trading know-how to make markets on legal sports betting exchanges.

The firm hired 67 roles via the H-1B program last year, including 24 software developers.

Tower Research Capital
----------------------

![Tower Research office in New York]()


Tower hired 67 employees via H-1B visa last year, including 29 quantitative traders and nine software engineers.
Tower Research / Glassdoor





**Headquarters:** New York


**Employees:** 1,000+

Tower Research has at times in its nearly 25-year history been among the [largest algorithmic traders](https://www.bloombergquint.com/onweb/mark-gorton-steps-down-as-ceo-of-hft-firm-tower-research-capital) in US and European stock markets. While privately owned, the firm operates like a multi-strategy hedge fund with individual, autonomous trading teams — a departure from competitors with flat, collaborative cultures.

The firm was cofounded in 1998 by Mark Gorton, who started out as an electrical engineer at a Lockheed Martin predecessor and later worked as a fixed-income prop trader at Credit Suisse First Boston. Gorton has always maintained interests outside of Wall Street, most notably as the founder of file-sharing service LimeWire, which rivaled Napster in the early 2000s and was likewise sued into submission by music record labels.


That means Gorton has more frequently found himself in the spotlight, an outspoken counterexample to his more secretive HFT peers. While some slinked further into the shadows after "Flash Boys" provoked a firestorm, Gorton [spoke out in the industry's defense](https://www.tradersmagazine.com/departments/people/the-hft-believer-mark-gorton-of-tower-research/). He stepped down as Tower CEO in 2019 but remains chairman of the firm.

Tower hired 67 employees via H-1B visa last year, including 29 quantitative traders and nine software engineers.
