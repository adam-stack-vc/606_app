**Maker-taker** is an exchange or trading platform pricing system. Its basic structure gives a transaction rebate to market makers providing liquidity (the makers); and charges a transaction fee to customers who take liquidity out of the market (the takers).

Firms that "make" a trade happen by posting buy and sell offers are paid a fee, typically between about 20 cents and 30 cents for every 100 shares traded. Firms that "take" those shares are charged a fee. [[1]](#cite_note-1)

The maker-taker model is in contrast with the "customer priority" model. Under customer priority, any account identified as “customer” goes to the head of the queue for priority of fill, without paying a transaction fee to the exchange. The exchange stays in business by charging market makers fees for transactions and payment for [order flow](/wiki/Order_flow "Order flow"). The payment for order flow is paid to [brokerage](/wiki/Brokerage "Brokerage") firms as an inducement to send their [orders](/wiki/Order "Order") to a given exchange.[[2]](#cite_note-2)

History[[edit](/wiki/index.php?title=Maker-taker&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The maker-taker model in [options](/wiki/Options "Options") trading came from a pricing model that was used by the [equity](/wiki/Equity "Equity") market. Developed by [Island ECN](/wiki/Island_ECN "Island ECN") in 1997, it was designed by [Joshua Levine](/wiki/index.php?title=Joshua_Levine&action=edit&redlink=1 "Joshua Levine (page does not exist)") to give [liquidity](/wiki/Liquidity "Liquidity") providers an incentive to [trade](/wiki/Trade "Trade") in markets where [spreads](/wiki/Spread "Spread") are extremely tight. It rewards [liquidity](/wiki/Liquidity "Liquidity") providers by giving them rebates, and it charges customers who remove liquidity from the exchange. Makers would get a $0.002/share rebate and takers would pay a $0.003/share fee. The exchange would get the difference which was $0.001/share.[[3]](#cite_note-3) Maker-taker pricing is predicated on a penny-increment environment.[[4]](#cite_note-4)[[5]](#cite_note-5)

The maker-taker model was only adopted by the [options](/wiki/Options "Options") [exchanges](/wiki/Exchange "Exchange") fairly recently and has been controversial. After the [Chicago Board Options Exchange](/wiki/Chicago_Board_Options_Exchange "Chicago Board Options Exchange") (CBOE) got rid of customer transaction [fees](/wiki/Fees "Fees") in May 2000, the idea of passing fees along to the customer has been anathema in the options market. There was a fear in the industry that charging customers for access to liquidity would inhibit the market's growth.

[NYSE Euronext](/wiki/NYSE_Euronext "NYSE Euronext")'s [Arca Options](/wiki/Arca_Options "Arca Options") platform and [Nasdaq OMX Group](/wiki/Nasdaq_OMX_Group "Nasdaq OMX Group")'s NOM platform both use a "maker-taker" pricing system, as does the new options exchange launched by [BATS](/wiki/BATS "BATS"). The [Chicago Board Options Exchange](/wiki/Chicago_Board_Options_Exchange "Chicago Board Options Exchange") and the [International Securities Exchange](/wiki/International_Securities_Exchange "International Securities Exchange") both use the customer priority, payment-for-order-flow system.[[6]](#cite_note-6) However, in March 2010 the ISE introduced a maker-taker structure for options on the [PowerShares QQQ](/wiki/PowerShares_QQQ "PowerShares QQQ") [Exchange Traded Fund](/wiki/Exchange_Traded_Fund "Exchange Traded Fund"), [Citigroup, Inc.](/wiki/Citigroup,_Inc. "Citigroup, Inc."), and [Bank of America Corporation](/wiki/Bank_of_America_Corporation "Bank of America Corporation").

Criticism[[edit](/wiki/index.php?title=Maker-taker&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Criticism")]
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Critics of the maker-taker model have argued that the fees might be giving brokers incentives to prioritize maker-taker fees over trade execution quality. Subsequent to a study by [Woodbine Associates](/wiki/Woodbine_Associates "Woodbine Associates") which highlighted the potential conflict of interest, Senator [Charles Schumer](/wiki/Charles_Schumer "Charles Schumer") requested that the [Securities and Exchange Commission](/wiki/Securities_and_Exchange_Commission "Securities and Exchange Commission") study the issue.[[7]](#cite_note-7) In a speech April 2, 2014, SEC commissioner [Luis Aguilar](/wiki/Luis_Aguilar "Luis Aguilar") suggested the commission entertain "a pilot program in which maker-taker rebates would be temporarily prohibited for certain securities.”[[8]](#cite_note-8)

ICE's CEO Jeffrey Sprecher has said the maker-taker model distorts pricing because there are rebates and other discounts that do not show up in the bid/offer, and that the trading noise created by the model has crowded out the price discovery process.[[9]](#cite_note-9) Another criticism is that [high frequency traders](/wiki/High_frequency_traders "High frequency traders") could take advantage of rebates by buying and selling shares for the same price in order to make money on arbitrage. [[10]](#cite_note-10) [[11]](#cite_note-11)

In a June 17, 2014 hearing of the Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, [NYSE](/wiki/NYSE "NYSE") president [Thomas Farley](/wiki/Thomas_Farley "Thomas Farley") said the exchange is "seeking support for the elimination of maker-taker." At the same hearing, [Vanguard](/wiki/Vanguard "Vanguard")'s [Joseph Brennan](/wiki/Joseph_Brennan "Joseph Brennan") also called for a "revisit" of maker-taker.[[12]](#cite_note-12)
