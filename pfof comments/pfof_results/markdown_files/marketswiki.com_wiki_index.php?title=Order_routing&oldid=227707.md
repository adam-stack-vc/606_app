Order routing is the process by which an [order](/wiki/Order "Order") goes from the [end user](/wiki/End_user "End user") to an [exchange](/wiki/Exchange "Exchange"). An order may go directly to the exchange from the customer, or it may go first to a [broker](/wiki/Broker "Broker") who then routes the order to the exchange.

"Smart" order routing attempts to achieve best execution of [trades](/wiki/Trades "Trades") while minimizing market impact. It is designed to help firms in an increasingly fragmented market to search for hidden liquidity, find opportunities in dark pools and use algorithms to maximize results without moving the market. [[1]](#cite_note-1)

In [stock](/wiki/Stock "Stock") trading, it is a common practice among brokerage firms to route orders to certain [market makers](/wiki/Market_makers "Market makers"), who then "rebate" 1 to 4 cents per share back to the brokerage firm in exchange for the directed order flow. This practice is known as "[payment for order flow](/wiki/Payment_for_order_flow "Payment for order flow")" (PFOF). [[2]](#cite_note-2)
